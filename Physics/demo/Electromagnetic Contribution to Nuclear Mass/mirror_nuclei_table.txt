
Mirror-nuclei mass differences: uniform-charged-sphere model vs experiment.
================================================================================

For a mirror pair (Z, N) <-> (N, Z), A = Z + N, the nuclear mass difference is

    dm = m_nuc(N protons) - m_nuc(Z protons)
       = dE_C  -  (N - Z) (m_n - m_p) c^2                              [eV]

with the electrostatic term taken from the uniformly charged sphere,
E_C = (3/5) k Q^2 / R, and pairwise (i<j) counting Z(Z-1) rather than Z^2:

    dE_C = (3/5) * [e / (4 pi eps0)] * (N - Z)(A - 1) / R_avg          [eV]

since  N(N-1) - Z(Z-1) = (N - Z)(A - 1).

Note the single power of e: energies are reported in eV, and eV is numerically
the electrostatic potential in volts, so e/(4 pi eps0) = 1.44e6 V*fm is the
whole constant needed when R is in fm.

R is the EQUIVALENT UNIFORM-SPHERE radius, not the rms charge radius.  For a
uniform sphere  r_rms = sqrt(3/5) R,  so  R = sqrt(5/3) * r_rms.  Skipping this
conversion is worth a factor of 1.29 and is the single most common way to get
this calculation wrong.

The residual is NOT the Nolen-Schiffer anomaly.  NS is a ~7% SHORTFALL of
careful microscopic calculations against experiment.  The raw uniform
sphere here runs ~3% HIGH because it omits the Coulomb exchange term, the
finite proton charge distribution, and the c.m. correction -- all of which
reduce the computed dE_C by several percent.  Apply those and this model
would land below experiment, which is where NS lives.

The largest outliers are structural, not statistical: 13N, 17F and 41Sc are
exactly the cases where the odd proton occupies a new shell just outside a
closed core (12C, 16O, 40Ca).  That proton orbits well outside the charge
radius of the core, so it feels less repulsion than a uniformly smeared
fluid would predict, and the model over-shoots.  17F is the extreme case:
its valence 2s1/2 proton is famously halo-like.

If the electromagnetic term is dropped entirely and only
(Z-N)(m_n - m_p) is kept, EVERY mirror pair comes out with the wrong
sign: the proton-rich member is predicted lighter, when it is heavier.
  cases with correct sign, naive term alone : 1 / 29
  cases with correct sign, full formula     : 29 / 29

Using each member's own radius is EXACT within the model and yet agrees
WORSE.  The reason is error amplification, not physics.  For Fe-54/Ni-54
the two absolute Coulomb energies are ~118 and ~135 MeV; their difference
is 17 MeV.  A 1% error in either radius -- from the measurement, or from
the uniform-sphere idealisation itself -- moves a 130 MeV number by 1.3
MeV, i.e. by 7% of the answer.  Holding R fixed makes that systematic
cancel in the subtraction.  The equal-radius assumption is therefore not
just a convenience: it is the numerically stable way to use this model.
(The real radius difference is a genuine, measured effect -- the extra
proton's repulsion inflates the proton-rich partner -- and is used to
constrain the symmetry-energy slope L.  It is simply too small to extract
through a formula this crude.)
